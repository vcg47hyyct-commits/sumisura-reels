## Evidence (PubMed)
- **Scoon et al., 2007** (J Sci Med Sport, crossover, 6 male distance runners): humid sauna ~90°C for ~31 min right after
  training, ~13 sessions in 3 weeks. Run time to exhaustion +32% (90% CL 21-43%), equal to ~1.9% (1.3-2.4%) in a time trial.
  Plasma volume +7.1%. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsams.2006.06.009 — evidence: **moderate** (real effect, very small sample).
- **Kirby et al., 2021** (Eur J Appl Physiol, 20 middle-distance runners, 13 female): ~28 min post-exercise sauna 3x/week for
  3 weeks: lower heart rate and core temp in a heat test, VO2max and speed at 4 mmol lactate improved vs control.
  https://doi.org/10.1007/s00421-020-04541-z — **moderate**, supports the same protocol. Not used on screen yet (next batch).
- **Ahokas et al., 2025** (Sports Med Open, systematic review, 14 studies): post-exercise heat "may improve running performance,
  at least in hot conditions"; no effect on cycling performance; evidence low to moderate.
  https://doi.org/10.1186/s40798-025-00910-0 — reason to keep "small study" in every caption.
- Last 30 days: Miliotis et al., Oct 2026 (Sports Med, narrative review): post-exercise heat before sleep may help sleep.
  https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-026-02555-9 — possible later angle, weak evidence.

## Social / skeptical
- Popular coverage (Tom's Guide and sauna vendors) sells the 32% as "run 32% faster". Wrong: it is time to exhaustion;
  the race estimate is ~1.9%. Gap for Sumisura: say both numbers honestly.
